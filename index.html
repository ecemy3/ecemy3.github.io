CASE STUDY · CALLMETRIC AI

In-Vehicle Speech Emotion Recognition

Speech emotion recognition framed as continuous valence–arousal (VA) prediction rather than categorical classification. The focus wasn't just accuracy — it was robustness under real-world conditions.

What I did
01
Encoder benchmarking

Compared transformer-based audio encoder architectures — Whisper, Wav2Vec2 and emotion-specific pretrained backbones.

02
Noise robustness

Designed noise-augmentation training strategies for robustness to real-world background noise, and systematically analyzed the clean-accuracy vs. noise-robustness trade-off.

03
Cross-lingual generalization

Evaluated zero-shot cross-lingual generalization across English and French.

04
End-to-end model selection

Built the selection process around CCC (concordance correlation coefficient) evaluation, freeze-depth ablation sweeps, and latency / footprint benchmarking.

Key lesson
A model being “good on clean data” and being “production-reliable” are very different claims. Closing that gap takes rigorous ablation work — and the discipline to report honest negative results, not just the wins.

CASE STUDY · CALLMETRIC AI

Call-Center Emotion Analytics & Live Coaching

A real-time call analysis system combining speech-to-text with emotion understanding for customer service operations.

How it works
Live call audio→Turkish ASR→Diarization→Emotion trajectory→Live coaching

Calls are transcribed using a fine-tuned Turkish ASR model, with speaker diarization separating customer and agent turns. On top of the transcript, an emotion analysis layer tracks the emotional trajectory of both parties throughout the call — surfacing moments of frustration, escalation or de-escalation as they happen, not just in a post-call summary.

The live-coaching component turns this into an operational tool: agents receive real-time signals during the call, so they can adjust tone or approach in the moment instead of learning about a bad interaction after the fact.

Key technical pieces
01
Fine-tuned Turkish Whisper

Domain-specific fine-tuning for transcription accuracy on real call-center Turkish.

02
Speaker diarization

Attributes the emotional signal correctly between customer and agent.

03
Streaming inference pipeline

Real-time analysis during the call — not just batch / offline processing.

04
Actionable emotion modeling

Designed to drive decisions mid-call, not only to describe what happened afterwards.

BACHELOR'S THESIS · KOCAELI UNIVERSITY · 2026

From Music to Visuals: Music Emotion Recognition & Emotion-to-Art Engine

A comparative study of human vs. AI emotion perception in music — predicting continuous valence–arousal from audio with deep learning, benchmarking against human listeners and a multimodal LLM, and turning emotion profiles into generative abstract art.

1,802tracks · DEAM
298survey participants
0.81RMSE · weighted ensemble
0.73 / 0.79Pearson · valence / arousal
Pipeline
DEAM audio→Mel-spectrograms→CNN · CRNN · Transformer→Weighted ensemble
Human survey→VA space→Gemini 2.5 Flash→Emotion-to-Art→FLUX.1 artwork
What I did
01
Model benchmarking

Trained CNN, CRNN (CNN + BiLSTM), Audio Transformer and CNN–Transformer hybrids on mel-spectrograms (1,261 / 271 / 270 split). 24+ controlled experiments across losses (MSE, MSE + CCC, SmoothL1), SpecAugment, dropout, learning rate and weight decay — evaluated with RMSE, MAE, Pearson and CCC.

02
Ensembling

Compared simple-average, weighted and stacking ensembles. The weighted ensemble reached an average RMSE of 0.8136 vs. 0.8311 for the best single model and was chosen for the live system.

03
Human vs. AI

Ran an online survey with 298 participants, normalized Likert ratings into the valence–arousal space, and compared humans against CNN, CRNN, the ensemble and Gemini 2.5 Flash. Human–model distance: 0.91; human–Gemini: 1.32 — Gemini captured the overall mood but often misread energy (arousal).

04
Emotion-to-Art Engine

Builds an Emotion Profile from dominant emotions, VA means, intensity, diversity, Shannon entropy and positive/negative ratios, then derives Art DNA, Color DNA and Composition DNA to prompt FLUX.1-schnell. E.g. Adagio for Strings → peace 29%, joy 12%, melancholy 10% → the artwork on the card.

Honest findings
Transformers underperformed CNNs here — DEAM is likely too small for their data appetite — and the CNN–Transformer hybrid was the weakest model. Combining model families in an ensemble beat every single architecture.
